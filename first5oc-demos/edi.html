Early Development Index (EDI)

Orange County’s 2025 Early Development Index

Using data to create systems change in Orange County through policy change, targeted intervention, and will building.

A picture of communities, not individual children. The EDI is a population-level measure of how groups of young children are developing. It is never used to screen, identify or diagnose an individual child.

How to use the map

    Darker shades mean lower levels of kindergarten readiness.

    How the map protects families

    • Children are mapped by where they live, not the school they attend.
    • Results are shown in aggregate. Individual children are never reported.
    • Neighborhoods with fewer than 10 valid EDI records are suppressed for confidentiality.

    Can’t use the map? Request neighborhood data or help.

    What the EDI measures

    Five developmental domains

    Kindergarten teachers complete the EDI for each child during the second half of the school year. Results show the share of kindergartners on track in each area.

    Reading the results

    How EDI results are classified

    In each of the five areas, every child’s results fall into one of three groups. Children who are on track in all five areas are considered ready for kindergarten.

    On track

    Above the 25th percentile.

    At risk

    Above the 10th percentile and at or below the 25th percentile.

    Not on track / vulnerable

    At or below the 10th percentile.

    The EDI also includes 16 developmental subareas, reported as ready, somewhat ready or not ready. Area and subarea classifications are not interchangeable.

      How the data is collected

      Kindergarten teachers receive online training and complete an EDI record for each child in their classroom. EDI data is collected across every Orange County elementary school district and from some charter schools.

      The EDI was developed by Dan Offord and Magdalena Janus at the Offord Centre for Child Studies at McMaster University in Canada. The UCLA Center for Healthier Children, Families and Communities holds the U.S. implementation license and partners with First 5 Orange County.
