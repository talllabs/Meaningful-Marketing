How to get services

Anyone can ask us for help. It is free to apply. We will walk with you through each step.

How old is the person who needs help?

Steps for people age 3 and older

  1. Contact us

    Call our Intake team or fill out a short online form. You can do this yourself, or a family member, doctor or teacher can help.

  2. Meet with us

    We meet with you to learn about the person, their health and their daily life. Bring any records you have. It's OK if you don't have any.

  3. We check if you can get services

    We may do a free assessment. This helps us understand if the person has a developmental disability that qualifies for services.

  4. Make a plan together

    If you qualify, you get a Service Coordinator. Together you make a plan for the services and support that fit your goals.

Steps for babies and toddlers (Early Start)

  1. Contact us

    If you are worried about how your baby is growing, moving, talking or learning, call us. You don't need a doctor's note.

  2. Free evaluation

    We look at how your child is developing. This is free.

  3. Make a family plan

    If your child qualifies, we make a plan with you. It is called an Individualized Family Service Plan (IFSP).

  4. Get support at home or nearby

    Services like speech or physical therapy can start right away, often in your home.

Need help in another language? We offer free interpreters, including American Sign Language. Just ask when you contact us.

Words you might hear

Service Coordinator
Your main contact at Westside. They help you find and plan services.
IPP (Individual Program Plan)
Your written plan. It lists your goals and the services that help you reach them.
IFSP
The plan for babies and toddlers in Early Start.
Service provider
A person or company that gives services, like therapy or respite care.
Lanterman Act
A California law. It says people with developmental disabilities have the right to services and support.
