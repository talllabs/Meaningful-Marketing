Here are some logo ideas

A few early directions for carrying WORTH’s energy forward, and one way it could come to life on your website. Just to show you some of the things we’re thinking.

Current identity

The original

The handwritten name, circular badge and vivid gradient give WORTH a recognizable, approachable identity.

The refresh opportunity is to give the name more authority and make the logo easier to use across a website header, a summit stage and a partner deck.

Five logo ideas

01 / The closest evolution

Refined Badge

One ring and cleaner curved type let the familiar handwriting and color do the work.

Supports continuity across partner materials and existing event signage. The curved descriptor still needs room.

02 / Keep the handwriting

Rising Signature

The handwritten name takes center stage, with a rising stroke and a straight descriptor creating a more flexible horizontal shape.

Suited to an approachable, event-led identity. The lettering remains informal for institutional settings.

03 / Lead with impact

Editorial Sans

Heavy, condensed capitals and a slight forward lean bring the energy of a campaign headline.

Suited to banners, posters and apparel. The italic stance gives it a sportier feel.

04 / Connect through color

Bold Ribbon

Weightier, sculptural serifs give WORTH more presence. The interwoven orange-to-pink ribbon carries the idea of connection into the lettering itself.

The heavier lettering gives the mark presence on summit signage and in partner decks.

05 / Put color in the lettering

Two-Tone O

Bold, sculpted serifs give WORTH presence. The O carries the original orange and pink in two clean color blocks.

Offers a flexible digital identity. The O could stand on its own as a favicon or social avatar.

See it on a website

A mockup with one of the ideas

To show you how a new logo could feel in use, we put idea 04, Bold Ribbon, on a sample WORTH homepage. The ribbon weaves through the headline the way it weaves through the logo, then threads between sections down the page.

It’s an early sketch to share some of the things we’re thinking. It likely won’t end here, and we’d love your reactions.
