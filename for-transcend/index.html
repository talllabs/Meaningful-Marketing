The approach

Wins first. Big build later.

Improve what already works, reduce manual effort and outside costs where possible, and test the highest-value ideas. Along the way we get a much clearer view of what a larger Phase 2 should include, before anyone commits to a large multi-year technology project.

1

Improve what's working

Keep the tools that earn their place. Connect them better instead of replacing everything at once.

2

Cut manual work

Fewer touch points, less re-typing and less relying on outside consultants to make tools behave.

3

Test the big ideas

Put real versions in front of your team and clients and let usage tell us what matters.

4

Plan Phase 2 with facts

Decide what to integrate, simplify or engineer more deeply once we have seen it working.

What we heard

Customization is the product.

Transcend is built around flexibility: full-ship charters for incentives, meetings, brand activations, affinity groups and celebrations. One ship is sailing, three more are coming, and the team is doing a lot of the connecting work by hand.

The website is a placeholder

It looks good, but it does not help a Kia, a Flexjet or a broker quickly see where they fit.

The tools are a quilt

Each system does its job, but information gets re-entered between them and things get stuck.

The calendar is hard to keep current

With low water and rerouting, keeping deployment information up to date is a constant effort.

Clients need one place to go

Manifests, assets, proposals and approvals are spread across email and attachments.
