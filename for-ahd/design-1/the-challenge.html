The challenge

Over a third of the world’s population lacks access to quality health care.

In rural Ecuador, the nearest hospital is often hours away in a major city.

Barriers at every step.

Rural Ecuadorian populations face a wide range of health care issues rooted in social, economic and political determinants. A major issue is the lack of access to high-quality, reliable health care.

Outpatient facilities are inaccessible and the nearest hospitals are typically hours away, forcing sick villagers to overcome geographic, economic and cultural barriers to seek care. Those who are willing and able to pay would much prefer to receive quality care in their own community, if such facilities were available.

Who is most at risk

Primary care has accomplished much. It isn’t enough.

Mothers

Women continue to suffer, and often die, from complications of pregnancy.

Children

Children continue to die at an alarming rate from infectious diseases.

An aging population

Diabetes, heart disease and arthritis go untreated because most rural health workers can’t manage chronic illness.

Fewer than half of doctors in Ecuador have residency training, and the vast majority of those physicians live and work in the two major cities.
