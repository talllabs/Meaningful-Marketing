From the Field

By Dr. David Gaus

As we plan how to provide health care services in the Cotopaxi province, we think about the characteristics of the population. We know in medicine and public health that a cookie-cutter approach to patient care is not a wise strategy. Just as each patient requires a tailored approach for their life circumstances, communities have their own unique circumstances and context.

You have heard us mention the large indigenous population in Cotopaxi. We recognize that their worldview can be different from the mestizo (mixed indigenous/Spanish) population, and we understand that how they view the world, or their cosmovision, greatly influences how they understand their own health. Frequently, western medicine practices pose a struggle or simply run counter to their constructs around health.

All too often western medicine practitioners (and health care systems) are less willing to understand a non-western cosmovision. Of course, there are examples where non-western health practices can place a patient directly in harm’s way, and those situations often provide little room for compromise. But at the same time, the doctor-patient negotiation process requires an acknowledgement of that belief system to gain patient trust.

In our own culture here in the US, non-western medical beliefs abound and we don’t even think about them because they are so ingrained: chicken soup for the common cold; going outside in cold weather with damp hair and no warm hat will get you sick. These beliefs have never been proven, or have actually been disproven, but we can’t let them go.

Equally interesting, elements of this non-western cosmovision embraced by significant indigenous populations also exist in the mestizo population. Why wouldn’t they?

“Meeting communities where they are is a fundamental competency of a health care system.”

We go into Cotopaxi Province with eyes wide open to these complexities. Meeting patients where they are is a fundamental competency of any physician. Meeting communities where they are, including the complexities around various perspectives of health, is a fundamental competency of a health care system. We look forward to that challenge in Cotopaxi.

Gratefully,

David

David Gaus, M.D., MPH/TM, is the Founder and CEO of Andean Health & Development.

Hospital Cotopaxi Is Taking Shape

Thanks to the support of so many AHD friends, we remain on track to open our new hospital in Cotopaxi, but there is still work to do!

 

The structural buildings are fully finished. The construction team is now focused on the interior finishes that are necessary and unique to a hospital. From a welcoming entrance to the lab rooms, we are excited by the progress we have made.

 

The team is now diligently working to secure the equipment needed to offer the highest quality care once our doors open.

2026 Bike Ride

From the Finger Lakes to Ecuador!

24Riders 369Miles $444KRaised

The 2026 Bike Ride was another memorable event. Led by our incredible AHCT, 24 riders each rode approximately 369 miles and climbed 17,788 feet while taking in the scenic Finger Lakes region of New York. Both 7-day and 3-day riders enjoyed gorgeous weather and contagious camaraderie. With the generous support of our riders and donors, we have already raised $444,000!

 

Thank you for years of incredible support for an event that does so much for AHD and the people of Ecuador!
