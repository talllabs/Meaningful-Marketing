01 · What we see

The challenge is clarity.

Workforce Development, Land Stewardship and Responsible Wood Production share one mission but serve very different people. A Crew Member, a lumber buyer, a municipal client and a donor should each understand Landforce fast, and know exactly where they belong.

1

Workforce Development is the thread

Not a silo. Stewardship and RWP are the pathways where people build skills and careers.

2

RWP needs to sell like a business

A customer-facing experience for buying wood that still connects back to the mission.

3

Organize the site by what people want

Work with Landforce. Hire environmental services. Buy wood. Support Landforce.

4

Impact everywhere

Not tucked away on a single impact page.

5

Free your staff from WordPress

Say what you want to say, not just what the page builder allows.

Where our head is at

Workforce development is the engine. Land stewardship and responsible wood production are where the training, jobs and environmental impact happen.

How they talk Homeboy Industries

One workforce mission, many businesses, each serving its own audience.

How they organize Greyston Bakery

A real business where buyers, job seekers and supporters each find their path fast.

How it looks Patagonia

Mission plus real products, on a beautiful site we can learn from.

How it feels Parks Project

Vibe-wise, a bit of a DNA match for Landforce.

The key insight: you don't need to walk every visitor through your org chart first. Let them enter through what they need. And design-wise? Giant real photography, crews shown as skilled craftspeople, finished landscapes, lumber, tools and hands. The work is so visual, it should be the brand.

02 · What you get

The plan, step by step.

We come in with a truly blank slate. We listen to your staff, Crew Members, partners and customers first. What we do know: your new brand will exude cool, because what you all do is really dang cool.

01

Discovery + listening

Audits, a kickoff workshop, 8–10 internal interviews, a Crew Member + alumni session, 4–6 external interviews and a quick email survey. Low lift for staff.

02

Brand architecture + naming

One system that carries three lines of work (including RWP) without looking like three organizations. Tested with your audiences.

03

A full brand toolkit

Positioning, messaging, visual identity, voice and a practical brand guide with editable, ready-to-use files.

04

Applications + social

Up to 6 priority pieces or templates, plus a social media system with up to 6 editable templates and staff training.

05

Website strategy + content

Sitemap, user journeys, wireframes tested with real users, and up to 20 priority pages written for you.

06

Photography

Two half-day shoots with a Pittsburgh-area photographer: winter indoor + spring outdoor. 60–80 edited images.

07

A custom-built website

Off WordPress, with staff dashboards, Salesforce/Mailchimp forms, an RWP product catalog and an impact section staff can edit.

08

Testing, launch + training

Checked on every device, accessible, secure, then live training for your editors and a 30-day warranty.

The fine print: responsibilities + exceptions

Landforce provides: a day-to-day lead; access to staff, stakeholders, participants and contact lists; existing brand files, photos and program info; platform access; Salesforce vendor coordination; photo releases; consolidated, timely feedback; and fact checks + final approvals.

Exceptions: English-only launch; printing, fabrication, installation, video and legal trademark clearance excluded; advanced RWP commerce and automated impact data are future phases.

Proprietary tools: none required. All project assets, code and files transfer to Landforce on final payment.

Conflicts of interest: none known. We'll comply with debarment/suspension certification, EEO, Title VI and other grant requirements in the final contract.

Support after launch: 30-day warranty, then $125/hr as needed with a 2-business-day response (same day for outages).
