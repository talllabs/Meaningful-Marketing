Why organizations move from WordPress

When the upkeep outweighs the upside.

Most teams do not leave WordPress because of one big problem. It is the steady build-up of small ones.

01

Plugin maintenance

Sites often rely on dozens of plugins for forms, SEO, sliders, caching and security. Updates can conflict with each other, and one abandoned plugin can hold everything back.

Features we build live directly in your site, not in a stack of add-ons.

02

Constant security updates

Core, theme and plugin updates arrive regularly, and outdated plugins are one of the most common ways WordPress sites get compromised.

A custom site has a far smaller surface to patch, and we keep it updated for you.

03

Page builders and technical debt

Layers of page-builder markup, shortcodes and custom CSS make pages heavy and editing fragile. Change one block and something else shifts.

Content is stored as clean, structured content that is easy to edit and fast to load.

04

Hosting and performance

Keeping a busy WordPress site fast often means premium hosting tiers, caching plugins and image optimization tools working together.

Modern hosting and lean code make speed the default.

05

Developer dependence

When staff are afraid of breaking something, every small change becomes a request to a developer, and updates slow down.

We build admin tools your communications team can use confidently.

06

A crowded dashboard

Menus for every plugin, settings no one remembers adding and warnings to dismiss make day-to-day administration harder than it should be.

Your team only sees the tools they actually use.

Is moving right for you?

An honest fit check.

WordPress may still be a good fit if:

  • A reliable developer or host already keeps your site updated and secure
  • Your site is mostly a blog or news publication that WordPress handles well
  • You rely on a plugin ecosystem, such as a shop, that is working for you

It may be time to move if:

  • Plugin or theme updates regularly break something
  • Most of your website budget goes to maintenance instead of improvement
  • Staff avoid editing because they are worried about breaking pages
  • The site is slow even with caching and better hosting

We are happy to tell you if staying put makes more sense.

What the WordPress migration covers

Nothing important gets left behind.

Your existing website stays live while your new one is built. Here is what we take care of along the way.

Content migration

We export your posts, pages, media, categories and custom fields, strip out shortcodes and page-builder markup, and bring everything across as clean, structured content.

URL redirects and SEO protection

WordPress creates many kinds of URLs, from permalinks and category archives to attachment pages and old ?p= links. We map them all, carry over titles and meta descriptions, and set up redirects so rankings and inbound links are protected.

Forms and integrations

Gravity Forms, Contact Form 7, WPForms and similar plugins are rebuilt as native forms, with their connections to email, Mailchimp, Salesforce or payment tools reconnected and tested.

Accessibility

Page builders often produce markup that is hard for screen readers. We rebuild to WCAG 2.2 AA standards and test with real assistive technology.

Training

Your editors learn the new admin before launch. Most teams find it simpler than the WordPress dashboard they are used to.

Launch support

We handle the DNS switch, watch for broken links and search errors after launch, and stay available for questions.

Ownership

No more mystery logins. We document every account and, where it fits, set them up in your organization’s name.

Side by side

WordPress vs. a custom website

Both approaches serve a purpose. The right choice depends on how much flexibility, ownership and customization your organization needs.

WordPress

  • Open-source platform with a huge ecosystem
  • Features added through themes and plugins
  • Core, theme and plugin updates to manage
  • Performance depends on hosting and caching setup
  • Flexible, but complexity can build up over time

Custom website

  • Features built directly into the site
  • No plugin stack to maintain
  • Updates and backups handled for you
  • Fast by default on modern hosting
  • Admin tools designed around your team
  • Client-owned code and accounts where applicable

Questions

WordPress migration FAQ

Will we lose our search rankings if we leave WordPress?

Not if the move is planned carefully. We map every important URL, set up redirects, carry over titles and meta descriptions, and monitor search performance after launch.

Can we keep our blog and news archive?

Yes. Posts, categories, images and authors can all be migrated, and old post URLs redirect to their new locations.

Do we still need plugins after moving?

No. The features you rely on, such as forms, SEO settings and galleries, are built into the new site, so there is no plugin list to keep updated.
